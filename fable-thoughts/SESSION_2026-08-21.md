# Session 2026-08-21 — technical analysis tested and closed; deal cancellation mechanism mapped

## Chart patterns do not predict on synthetics

`ta_multiscan.py`: 4 symbols x 5 timeframes x 6 strategies = **112 cells**, on 4M clean
ticks each (46-93 days per symbol).

Strategies: sr_reversal, fvg_fill, order_block, breakout, momentum, mean_revert.

**Every strategy runs twice** — on real ticks, and on the SAME step distribution shuffled
into random order. Identical volatility and fat tails, zero time structure. Only
real-minus-shuffled is evidence.

### Result: the t-statistics are a standard normal

| statistic | observed | null expectation |
|---|---:|---:|
| mean t | +0.027 | 0.000 |
| sd of t | 1.201 | 1.000 |
| max abs t | 3.4 | 3.1 (for 112 draws) |
| cells with abs t > 2 | 7 | 5.1 |

The `real` win rate sits at 47-54% in every cell, centred on 50%. The test is symmetric
(+R vs -R), so 50% IS the null. Strongest cell was R_75 300t breakout at t=+3.0, which is
exactly what 112 independent draws produce by chance.

**Chart patterns describe the shape of a random walk. They do not predict it.**

### Why raw hit rates fool people

On a pure Gaussian random walk with zero memory by construction, swing highs rejected
downward **77.7%** of the time across 10,574 tests. A level price just touched is a level
price is currently NEAR, so it gets re-touched and sometimes rejects. Any backtest that
reports a hit rate without a shuffled control is measuring walk geometry.

### Practical finding: the spread is the entire game at small timeframes

Breakeven win rate at a 0.035% round-trip spread (16.56 points at ~48,000), target = stop =
median bar range:

| bar size | breakeven WR |
|---|---:|
| 1 min | **63.27%** |
| 2 min | 58.90% |
| 5 min | 55.37% |
| 10 min | 53.73% |
| 30 min | 52.10% |

R shrinks faster than the spread, so scalping needs a 63% win rate before it earns a cent.

### And win rate is a free parameter

On a driftless walk with a spread, EV is always approximately -spread regardless of win
rate. Simulated: TP/SL of 1:10 gives a 90.7% win rate, 10:1 gives 9.7%, and both lose the
same amount. Win rate carries no information about profitability.

## MT5 adds nothing structural

- The Deriv MT5 API is **account management only** (creation, passwords, transfers). No
  trading, no market data. Automation needs the MT5 terminal and its own stack.
- **Swap arbitrage is dead**: Deriv offers swap-free accounts, and no overnight fees on
  selected Derived Indices. Zero on both sides means no carry.
- **The volatility drag is NOT tradeable.** JD100's -0.186%/day log-drift equals -sigma^2/2
  exactly. But that is Jensen's inequality: simulating 200k paths gives mean LOG return
  -8.42% while the mean PRICE is +0.12%. E[S_T] = S_0. A short position pays in price, so
  E[P&L] = 0, and minus the spread it is negative. Our own 46-day move of -8.15% is 0.20 sd
  from zero — consistent with a martingale.

## Deal cancellation: mechanism mapped, structure closed

`deal_cancel.py` + `cancel_pair_live.py`. The single leg is priced sensibly: without
cancellation the loss is already capped at the stake by the stop-out, so the option cannot
be worth more than the stake. On 1HZ100V mult 400/60m the fee is 7.62 against a ceiling of
10.00 (ratio 0.76).

The paired trade looked positive on paper (+18.69 EV) but live testing showed why not:

- with both legs alive, `sum` pinned at **-2.85 to -2.91 = -2 x commission**, poll after
  poll. **The mult_trunc identity holds in real time.**
- the losing leg hits stop-out and **auto-cancels with a full stake refund**
  (`status: cancelled`, profit -> +0.00), costing only the fee
- the survivor then runs as a naked directional position
- **both can knock out** if whipsawed — that happened in round 1

So it is a double-knockout straddle, not the `E|move|` straddle the model assumed. Live: 3
rounds at -8.24, -1.08, +4.49, mean **-1.61/round**, against a corrected model prediction of
-1.17 for that cell. Consistent.

# ADDENDUM 2026-08-28 — the information bound closes the entire search

`info_bound.py` on 4M clean JD100 ticks (46.3 days, 100% density). Mutual information
between the last k digits and the next digit, Miller-Madow corrected, against a shuffled
control at every k.

| k | contexts | raw MI | corrected | shuffled | excess |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 10 | 0.002196 | 0.002182 | 0.000003 | **+0.002179** |
| 2 | 100 | 0.002359 | 0.002198 | 0.000005 | **+0.002194** |
| 3 | 1,000 | 0.003805 | 0.002183 | 0.000000 | **+0.002183** |
| 4 | 10,000 | 0.018487 | 0.002258 | 0.000000 | **+0.002258** |

## The excess is FLAT in k

Four digits of history tell you exactly what one digit tells you. The process is first-order
at most, and that first order is the sigma-concentration effect already found. **There is no
higher-order structure of any kind.**

Note the raw column: 0.0022 -> 0.0038 -> 0.0185. Uncorrected, k=4 looks like an 8x
improvement. That is entirely estimator bias — plug-in MI is biased upward by roughly
(cells-1)/(2n ln2), which at k=6 alone would be 1.80 bits, 200x the amount needed for
breakeven. Any naive CTW or high-order MI analysis would report a spectacular edge made of
nothing.

## Why one number closes the whole class

By the data processing inequality, for any function g:

    I(g(past) ; next) <= I(past ; next)

So a neural net, context tree, signature transform, bispectrum, Volterra series,
deterministic-chaos reconstruction, or a perfect copy of the generator itself cannot exceed
the measured MI. All closed at once, without running any.

## What this retires

Against an external list of 15 proposed "gaps", this bound settles gaps 1, 3, 4, 12 and 13
outright. Separately already answered in-repo:

- **Kalman/particle filtering for sigma** — `sigma_from_spot.py` fits at 99.8% of the noise
  ceiling, residual/noise = 1.01. The residual IS the estimator's sampling error. Sigma is
  not latent; it is spot times a constant, observed exactly. Nothing to filter.
- **Portfolio optimisation across contracts** — violates linearity. E[X+Y] = E[X]+E[Y] under
  any dependence; diversification cuts variance, never sign. Same-tick digit contracts are
  mutually exclusive outcomes of one draw, not merely correlated.
- **Quantum Fisher information** — not applicable to a classical PRNG. The classical answer
  is known: the rolling estimator's error matches sigma/sqrt(2W), achieving the Cramer-Rao
  bound for its window.
- **Off-by-one entry timing** — `entry_tick_test.py` measured exit-entry = +0 on every
  round. Entry T+1, settlement T+1, horizon exactly 1 step. Tables were correct.
- **EVT/Hawkes on jumps** — jumps are irrelevant regardless: conditioning on "next tick is
  not a jump" with perfect hindsight moves the win rate 0.018pp.

## Genuinely still untested

- **MT5 feed vs API feed lead-lag.** Two feeds for the same underlying, never compared
  tick-by-tick. If they differ by even 100ms it is actionable, and it sits outside
  everything the information bound covers, because it is a latency question rather than a
  prediction question.

# ADDENDUM 2026-08-28b — feed lead-lag closed. Nothing structural remains.

The information bound closes every model that is a function of the past. The one hypothesis
it cannot touch is INFRASTRUCTURE ASYMMETRY: if two feeds deliver the same tick at different
times, the leading one shows the future of the lagging one, and no argument about the price

## The cross-gateway version has no premise

`deriv_api.py` uses exactly one endpoint:

    wss://api.derivws.com/trading/v1/options/ws/public

There is no second gateway to race. (The first version of `feed_race.py` invented two URLs —
`ws.binaryws.com` timed out and a guessed `/websockets/v3` path returned 404. Reading the
source would have taken one `Select-String`. Same failure as the `start` parameter, the

## The per-connection version measures something real

Two independent sockets to the same endpoint, R_75, 180s, 91 matched epochs:

| statistic | value |
|---|---:|
| mean delta (A − B) | **+93.8 ms** |
| median | +100.6 ms |
| sd | 37.9 ms |
| socket_B first | **96.7%** of ticks |
| t | **+23.6** |

Prices agree exactly on every matched epoch, so this is delivery timing, not data.

Probably a client artifact: both sockets are Python threads sharing a GIL, `socket_A`
connects first, and `socket_B` leads. A second connection winning by a consistent 100 ms
points at thread scheduling or Cloudflare routing the two sockets to different backends,
rather than the server servicing subscribers unevenly. Swapping connection order would
settle it.

## But it is structurally unusable at ANY lead size

A feed lead tells you about a tick that has ALREADY OCCURRED on the server. The timing on a
1-tick digit contract is fixed and was measured directly by `entry_tick_test.py`:

    buy at time t  ->  entry = tick T+1  ->  settlement = tick T+1

To profit from knowing T+1 you would have to buy after seeing it. But a buy placed after
T+1 gets entry T+2, which is unknown. **The lead delivers information on the wrong side of
the entry boundary.**

This holds at any magnitude — 500 ms would not help. The "smaller than the 137 ms round
trip" framing in the tool's own verdict is too weak; the correct close is structural, not
numerical.

# ADDENDUM 2026-08-29 — intermittency tested; the bound is 0.42pp from the price

## An external review proposed 26 cross-disciplinary "gaps". One was real.

Sixteen were functions g(past) — Koopman modes, random matrix spacing, p-adic valuations,
RG coarse-graining, spin-glass overlaps, subword complexity, compressed sensing, path
integrals, percolation, Malliavin Greeks, quasicrystal diffraction, multifractals, ETAS,
nucleation, Wasserstein geodesics, information geometry. All bounded by
I(g(past); next) <= I(past; next). They can DETECT structure; they cannot exceed the bound.

Seven were already in the repo. Eight were metaphors with no falsifiable prediction.

**The survivor: intermittency.** 0.0023 bits is a SAMPLE AVERAGE. If predictability
concentrates in rare windows, trading only those is not bounded by the mean. That attacks
the averaging itself rather than proposing another predictor, and it was untested.

## Intermittency is real, and it is sigma

`mi_intermittency.py` on 4M JD100 ticks found windowed MI 5-173x wider than shuffled, 45 of
691 windows above the bit requirement, and half-to-half persistence of 0.64-0.88.

`mi_vs_sigma.py` identified the cause. Over 46 days spot ran 184.66-274.03, so sigma ran
3.35-4.76, and MI depends on sigma by construction:

| | value |
|---|---:|
| corr(sigma, windowed MI) | **-0.8691** |
| corr(wrapped-normal prediction, measured MI) | **+0.9739** |
| mean predicted / measured | 0.003242 / 0.002808 |

The wrapped normal predicts the entire observed MI range from sigma alone, with no fitting.

## The "second state variable" was my own misspecification

The first pass regressed MI on sigma LINEARLY. But MI(sigma) is strongly convex — 0.0255 at
sigma 3.2 down to 0.00016 at 4.8. A straight line through a convex curve leaves systematic
residuals at both ends, and because sigma persists within a window both halves inherit the
same residual sign, manufacturing fake persistence.

Refitting on the parameter-free wrapped-normal prediction:

| | linear in sigma | wrapped normal |
|---|---:|---:|
| R^2 | 0.7553 | **0.9484** |
| residual sd | 0.001475 | 0.000677 |
| residual / noise | 6.63 | 2.40 |
| **persistence h1-h2** | **+0.4176** | **+0.0579** |

Persistence was the load-bearing claim and it went to zero. The remaining excess variance
(2.40x noise) has no persistence, so it is unpredictable in advance and untradable — most
likely within-window sigma variation, since one realized sigma is assigned per 25,000-tick
window while MI depends on the sigma PATH inside it. The fit coefficient of 0.9048 matches
JD100's known -0.00124 offset from the wrapped normal.

**Third instance of the same error in this project**: through-origin sigma fit, raw plug-in
MI at k=4, and now linear-in-sigma. Wrong functional form read as signal each time.
