# Session 2026-08-21 — technical analysis tested and closed; deal cancellation mechanism mapped

## Chart patterns do not predict on synthetics

`ta_multiscan.py`: 4 symbols x 5 timeframes x 6 strategies = **112 cells**, on 4M clean
ticks each (46-93 days per symbol).

Strategies: sr_reversal, fvg_fill, order_block, breakout, momentum, mean_revert.

**Every strategy runs twice** — on real ticks, and on the SAME step distribution shuffled
into random order. Identical volatility and fat tails, zero time structure. Only
real-minus-shuffled is evidence.

### Result: the t-statistics are a standard normal

| statistic | observed | null expectation |
|---|---:|---:|
| mean t | +0.027 | 0.000 |
| sd of t | 1.201 | 1.000 |
| max abs t | 3.4 | 3.1 (for 112 draws) |
| cells with abs t > 2 | 7 | 5.1 |

The `real` win rate sits at 47-54% in every cell, centred on 50%. The test is symmetric
(+R vs -R), so 50% IS the null. Strongest cell was R_75 300t breakout at t=+3.0, which is
exactly what 112 independent draws produce by chance.

**Chart patterns describe the shape of a random walk. They do not predict it.**

### Why raw hit rates fool people

On a pure Gaussian random walk with zero memory by construction, swing highs rejected
downward **77.7%** of the time across 10,574 tests. A level price just touched is a level
price is currently NEAR, so it gets re-touched and sometimes rejects. Any backtest that
reports a hit rate without a shuffled control is measuring walk geometry.

### Practical finding: the spread is the entire game at small timeframes

Breakeven win rate at a 0.035% round-trip spread (16.56 points at ~48,000), target = stop =
median bar range:

| bar size | breakeven WR |
|---|---:|
| 1 min | **63.27%** |
| 2 min | 58.90% |
| 5 min | 55.37% |
| 10 min | 53.73% |
| 30 min | 52.10% |

R shrinks faster than the spread, so scalping needs a 63% win rate before it earns a cent.

### And win rate is a free parameter

On a driftless walk with a spread, EV is always approximately -spread regardless of win
rate. Simulated: TP/SL of 1:10 gives a 90.7% win rate, 10:1 gives 9.7%, and both lose the
same amount. Win rate carries no information about profitability.

## MT5 adds nothing structural

- The Deriv MT5 API is **account management only** (creation, passwords, transfers). No
  trading, no market data. Automation needs the MT5 terminal and its own stack.
- **Swap arbitrage is dead**: Deriv offers swap-free accounts, and no overnight fees on
  selected Derived Indices. Zero on both sides means no carry.
- **The volatility drag is NOT tradeable.** JD100's -0.186%/day log-drift equals -sigma^2/2
  exactly. But that is Jensen's inequality: simulating 200k paths gives mean LOG return
  -8.42% while the mean PRICE is +0.12%. E[S_T] = S_0. A short position pays in price, so
  E[P&L] = 0, and minus the spread it is negative. Our own 46-day move of -8.15% is 0.20 sd
  from zero — consistent with a martingale.

## Deal cancellation: mechanism mapped, structure closed

`deal_cancel.py` + `cancel_pair_live.py`. The single leg is priced sensibly: without
cancellation the loss is already capped at the stake by the stop-out, so the option cannot
be worth more than the stake. On 1HZ100V mult 400/60m the fee is 7.62 against a ceiling of
10.00 (ratio 0.76).

The paired trade looked positive on paper (+18.69 EV) but live testing showed why not:

- with both legs alive, `sum` pinned at **-2.85 to -2.91 = -2 x commission**, poll after
  poll. **The mult_trunc identity holds in real time.**
- the losing leg hits stop-out and **auto-cancels with a full stake refund**
  (`status: cancelled`, profit -> +0.00), costing only the fee
- the survivor then runs as a naked directional position
- **both can knock out** if whipsawed — that happened in round 1

So it is a double-knockout straddle, not the `E|move|` straddle the model assumed. Live: 3
rounds at -8.24, -1.08, +4.49, mean **-1.61/round**, against a corrected model prediction of
-1.17 for that cell. Consistent.
