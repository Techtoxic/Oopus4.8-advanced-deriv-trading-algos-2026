# ADDENDUM 2026-08-20 — the drag rate was overstated 3.6x, and history goes back 46 days

## The API history limit is not 1.2M

Earlier notes recorded a ~1.2M tick ceiling. That was simply the largest request ever made,
not a limit. `fetch_ticks(ws, "JD100", 4_000_000)` returns **4,000,000 unique ticks spanning
46.30 days at 100% density**, zero duplicates. There may be no practical wall.

## The drag rate that drove every timeline was wrong

The sigma-decay thesis rested on a drift of -0.670%/day. Measured over 46 days:

| quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| spot | 241.33 -> 221.65 (-8.15%) |
| realised drift | **-0.2159%/day** |
| SE of drift | 0.8984%/day |
| t | **-0.24** |
| 95% CI | [-1.977%, +1.545%] per day |

Drift is not distinguishable from zero even at 46 days. But the theoretical drag is NOT a
free parameter — for GBM, d(log S)/dt = -sigma^2/2:

    measured daily vol 6.0935%  ->  implied drag -0.1857%/day
    realised drift                              -0.2159%/day   (0.03 SE apart)
    predicted 46-day move -8.2%  vs  actual -8.2%

**The physics is confirmed exactly. The rate was overstated 3.6x** because earlier work
assumed a daily vol near 11.6% when it is 6.09%, and drag goes as vol SQUARED.

Rolling 14-day estimates on the same series range **-1.783% to +1.481%/day**, with 39% of
windows showing spot RISING. The -0.67% figure was one draw from that distribution, and any
two-week sample can produce almost any "drag rate".

## What DID hold up

The sigma law, and now across a far wider range:

    14-day fit: sigma = 0.03982 + 1.746764e-04*spot   ->  gate 3.48 at spot 196.95
    46-day fit: sigma = 0.06887 + 1.735087e-04*spot   ->  gate 3.48 at spot 196.60
    R^2 0.95335 over 2222 blocks, spot 186.18-272.25, sigma 3.169-4.949

Constants stable to 0.2% on the gate spot. The sigma=f(spot) relationship is solid; only
the TIMING was wrong.

## Bottom line

This does not reopen the book — that closed on three payout cuts, not on data. But it
corrects the record: waiting for sigma was never a matter of days, and the correct framing
