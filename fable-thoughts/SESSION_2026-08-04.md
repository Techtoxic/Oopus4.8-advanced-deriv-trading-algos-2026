# CORRECTION (same day, later) — most of the above was computed on LOOPED TICKS

## The bug

`deriv_api.history_paged()` silently loops. Asked for 1.6M ticks of JD100 it returned
**86,401 unique epochs — 5.44%**, the same day repeated ~18 times (min inter-tick gap
-86401s).

Cause: `ticks_history` takes an optional `start` parameter which, for `style=ticks`,
**defaults to one day ago**. Paging with only `end` set meant that once `end` moved earlier
than that implicit default, the requested window was empty and the server returned the
latest data again. Sending an explicit `start` fixes it — verified at **1,200,000 unique
ticks over 13.89 days, 100% density**.

`derivfetch.py` is now the only correct fetcher and raises rather than returning degenerate
data.

## What it invalidated

chi-square scales linearly with replication, so:

| reported | replication | true | null mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| randomness_battery test C = 661.5 | 18.4x | 36.0 | 81 |
| battery_v2 = 675.6 | 6.9x | 98.0 | 81 |

On clean data the entry/offset chi2 is **67.6 on 81 df** — below the null mean, consistent
with independence. **The "z = +47 dependence" was entirely replication.**

Wilson intervals were too narrow by sqrt(k): 2.1x on 400k runs, 3.0x on 800k, 3.7x on
regime_revalidate's 1.2M. IS/OOS splits put copies of the same ticks on both sides.

Only `cross_symbol.py` (60k, under the cap) was unaffected — its independence result stands
as written.

# STEP INDEX RISE+FALL HEDGE — closed, and Deriv prices it exactly

A user proposed buying Rise and Fall simultaneously on Step Index. Worth taking seriously:
Step indices move a FIXED step every tick, so parity forbids ties on an odd number of steps
and one leg would ALWAYS win.

## The premise is real

`step_tie.py`, 300,000 unique stpRNG ticks: `|step| = 1 pip 100.0%`, zero flat ticks, 1s
interval, and P(tie) **exactly 0.00000** on 5/15/25 tick counts (0.246 / 0.176 / 0.145 on
10/20/30). Parity, not statistics.

## The off-by-one that kills it

`step_pair_test.py`, 20 real CALL+PUT pairs at 15s on demo:

- executed payout **2.4600** both legs (quote honest here, unlike JD100)
- entry->exit elapsed **14s every round**, never 15
- **7/20 rounds tied exactly** (d = +0.0), pair P&L **-20.05%/round**, total -8.02

A "15 second" contract spans **14 tick INTERVALS** — entry is the tick after purchase, exit
is 15 ticks later, leaving 14 gaps. 14 is EVEN, so ties are permitted. Duration D spans
(D-1) intervals.

## Deriv prices the exact binomial

`step_parity_scan.py`, durations 5-30s on all five step symbols. Identical on every symbol:

| k intervals | parity | quoted | fair 2/(1-P_tie) | true P(tie) | margin |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 14 | even | 2.4550 | 2.5300 | 0.2095 | 2.96% |
| 15 | odd | **1.9530** | 2.0000 | 0.0000 | 2.35% |
| 16 | even | 2.4170 | 2.4887 | 0.1964 | 2.88% |
| 17 | odd | **1.9530** | 2.0000 | 0.0000 | 2.35% |
| 20 | even | 2.3590 | 2.4278 | 0.1762 | 2.83% |
| 28 | even | 2.2870 | 2.3514 | 0.1494 | 2.74% |
| 29 | odd | **1.9530** | 2.0000 | 0.0000 | 2.35% |

Every odd k quotes **exactly 1.9530**. Every even k tracks `2/(1-P_tie)` where
`P_tie = C(k,k/2)/2^k`, with a 2.7-3.0% margin. They are not approximating — they are
computing the binomial coefficient.

## Verdict

Dead both ways:
- **odd** duration: one leg always wins, but 1.9530 < 2.0000 breakeven -> **-2.35%/round**
- **even** duration: 2.46 looks generous, but ties eat exactly the difference

The 20 live rounds at -20.05% match the model.

## Parity across ALL duration units — fully consistent

`step_units_scan.py` swept ticks 1-10, seconds 15-60, minutes 1-10 on stpRNG. The three
units map to interval counts differently:

    ticks   N  ->  N intervals          odd N (1,3,5,7,9) tie-free
    seconds D  ->  D-1 intervals         even D (16,18,20...) tie-free
    minutes M  ->  60M-1 intervals       ALWAYS odd, so always tie-free

**26 tie-free durations, every one quoting exactly 1.9530.** 13 tie-bearing durations with
mean |implied - true| P(tie) = 0.0240, which is simply the margin.

Ticks show the alternation most sharply: 2t has P(tie) = 0.5000 so fair is 4.0000 and Deriv
quotes 3.8170; 1t/3t/5t/7t/9t all quote 1.9530.

| duration | k | quoted | fair | margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| 29s | 28 | 2.2870 | 2.3514 | 2.74% |

### Why 26/26 exact is the finding

Ticks, seconds and minutes are almost certainly separate code paths — the seconds path even

tie-free duration. They compute `C(k,k/2)/2^k` correctly in each one.

**Step Index Rise+Fall is dead at every duration and every unit.** No further work needed.

# STRUCTURAL SEARCH COMPLETE

## The linearity argument closes every hold-to-expiry combination

E[X+Y] = E[X]+E[Y] under ANY dependence. So any combination of contracts held to expiry has
EV equal to the sum of the individual EVs. Verified directly on Step Index: mixing durations
gives exactly (EV_a + EV_b)/2 with no interaction term, and the best pair (-2.35%) merely
equals the least-bad single contract.

That single argument closes the whole family at once — different durations, different
symbols, staggered entries, N legs. No scan needed.

## Early close was the only exception, and it is priced

Linearity assumes FIXED payoffs. Early close breaks it: the payoff depends on when you act.

`sell_scan.py` mapped `is_valid_to_sell`:

| product | sellable | status |
|---|---|---|
| Step Index, all durations | NO | closed by linearity |
| JD100 / 1HZ100V @ 1 tick | flagged, but artifact | expires in 1s; flag catches settlement (bids 1.92/1.81 = final value) |
| **1HZ100V CALL 60s / 5m / 30m** | **YES** | the live case |
| MULT* | YES | already dead: paired long+short = -2*commission |
| ACCU | YES | already dead: G 0.9935-0.9979 |

`sellback_vol.py` priced the live case against fair value measured empirically — no model,
just `P(win) = P(S_k > -d)` from the observed distribution of k-tick displacements
(validated: d=0 returns 0.4815/0.4962/0.5035/0.5031 across horizons).

**567 quotes, mean bid/fair 0.8219 — a 17.81% spread.**

And the spread is shaped to defeat exactly what we were hunting:

| time remaining | bid/fair | | moneyness | bid/fair |
|---:|---:|---|---|---:|
| 240-298 | 0.9009 | | +108/+392 (deep ITM) | 0.9633 |
| 121-181 | 0.8652 | | -17/+108 | 0.9371 |
| 63-121 | 0.7962 | | -101/-17 | 0.8757 |
| **1-63** | **0.6476** | | **-323/-193 (deep OTM)** | **0.5206** |

They widen as expiry approaches and as the position goes against you — the two states where
early close would be worth most. Near expiry and deep OTM you receive barely half of fair.

One quote of 567 came in at ratio 1.0211 (d=-20, k=8). That is noise: the empirical CDF
snaps to the nearest sampled horizon at small k and carries real estimation error, and a
single point 2% above fair against a mean 18% below is not structure.

# ENTRY-TICK VERIFICATION — conditioning is correct, but latency now binds

## The assumption held

`entry_tick_test.py`, 20 x 1-tick DIGITOVER4 on JD100, bracketing each buy with
tick-history calls:

    exit - entry = +0 on EVERY round

Entry and exit are the SAME tick. So you condition on digit T, entry/settlement is T+1, and
the horizon is exactly ONE step — which is what the offset tables encode. The foundation of
the whole strategy is correct.

This was worth checking: step_pair_test showed a 15-SECOND contract spanning only 14
intervals, so entry is the tick after purchase. Had that meant T+1 entry and T+2 settlement
for digit contracts, the true horizon would have been 2 steps, where duration_surface
measured the conditioned lift at +4.54% versus +24.77% at 1 step. The thesis would have been
dead on arithmetic. It is not.

## But 6 of 20 rounds slipped to T+2

Median round trip 137ms against 1000ms tick spacing, with a tail to 906/355/339/330/325ms.
When a tick lands during the round trip, entry slips one further and you trade a 2-step
relationship instead of a 1-step one.

| sigma | EV at N=1 | eff. sigma at N=2 | EV at N=2 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3.95 (spot 223) | -2.75% | 5.59 | -14.31% |
| 3.56 | +0.01% | 5.03 | -10.41% |
| 3.48 | +0.58% | 4.92 | -9.61% |
| 3.30 | +1.85% | 4.67 | -7.82% |

**Maximum tolerable miss rate:**

| sigma | max miss rate |
|---:|---:|
| 3.56 | 0.1% |
| **3.48** | **5.7%** |
| 3.40 | 11.5% |
| 3.30 | 19.1% |
| 3.20 | 27.3% |

Breakeven spot moves from **198** at perfect execution to **172** at a 30% miss rate.

The 30% observed here is inflated by test design — the script made a `ticks_history` call
before buying and fired at arbitrary points in the tick cycle. A bot firing immediately on
tick receipt has ~860ms of margin against a 137ms median round trip. But the tail is real,
and at sigma 3.48 the budget is only 5.7%.

**Latency is now the binding constraint, not sigma.** adaptive_sentinel already logs a
`lag` column; that number must be monitored in production and the strategy gated on it.

## Also: spot reversed to 223

Spot moved from 209.96 to 223 (+6.2%) AGAINST the decay, taking sigma from 3.75 to 3.95.
This is the no-anchor risk. These indices are martingales: the median decays, the mean does
not.

    22 days: drift -0.147 in log terms, sd 0.543 -> P(spot < 192.29) = 50%
    90 days: drift -0.603, sd 1.099 -> P(spot < 192.29) = 66%

Noise is roughly four times the drift. The EV% = 25.18 - 7.07*sigma fit is solid physics,
but WHEN sigma arrives is a random walk, not a schedule. Earlier notes quoting "~12 days"
presented a median as a date. That was wrong and is retracted.
